**In an ideal world, security should be invisible, unnoticeable, flavourless, and odourless.** It should be there and does not disrupt users. However, in reality, security is very dependent on users. From choosing a quality password to spotting a phishing webpage, user awareness significantly influences the application security.

In the same vein, users do not want to get bothered with security restrictions. The online generation favour doing things fast rather than securely. Too much security will turn users away from a system or drive them to seek out loopholes. Relaxing security in favour of a better user experience (UX) can cause disaster in the long term.

There has been a trade-off between security and UX. There are many scenarios where it is hard to draw a conclusion and decide on the balance between security and UX. There is also no consistent answer to some of the UX designs even among top internet companies.

I have been frequently asked (and challenged) by our clients about the best ways to satisfy both UX and security. I should be honest to say that finding a middle ground has been a difficult task. When I listen to a UX designer, I have found there are many UX issues that we (the security community) do not understand and naturally, our security advice is not always fit for their purposes. Similarly, people in the UX community do not feel the importance of our security concerns and tend to favour UX over security.

Given this situation, I have decided to put together my understanding of UX designs and discuss how a not-well-designed UX can result in security vulnerabilities or misguide a user to follow bad security practices. I will be publishing a series of articles covering different UX designs - some of the information may be old and some may be new, however my goal is to give you an overview of issues around some common UX designs and be as comprehensive as possible.

For simplicity, I have categorised them into the following groups:

- UX vulnerabilities in the login process
- UX vulnerabilities in user registration
- UX vulnerabilities in password management
- UX vulnerabilities in user account management

I would like you to remember that, the topic is highly debatable and you may or may not agree with various points written. In many scenarios, there is no single best answer. Different systems have different threat models and companies have different risk appetites. In terms of recommendations, I have tried to cover advantages and disadvantages of different UX designs and I left it to a reader to choose what suits them best for their circumstances. I am not a UX expert and I am sure there are UX concerns that I have not considered. If you find any, I am happy to take it and improve on.

Before I close-off, let's read a definition for UX.

## What is UX?

User Experience, abbreviated: UX, is the quality of experience a person has when interacts with a specific design (sourced from allaboutux.org)

Effectively any front-end components that user sees and interacts to achieve a goal or perform a task is part of UX.
